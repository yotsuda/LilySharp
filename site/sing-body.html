What it does

A lyrics track in a Lily# score can be sung: press ▶ in the preview and a synthetic voice sings the words on their notes, in time with the other parts. You change a word or a note and the voice follows, singing again only the phrase you touched.

Singing is optional, and it comes from a separate free extension, Cantoria Singing Voice. Cantoria drives singing engines you install yourself; no voice comes with it. Everything runs on your own computer, and nothing is sent anywhere.

Install a voice

  1. In VS Code's Extensions view (Ctrl+Shift+X), search for Cantoria and install Cantoria Singing Voice. If you write a singer line without it, Lily# Studio offers to install it.
  2. Set up at least one of the three engines below. Each is its makers' work, downloaded from them: nothing here comes with Lily# or Cantoria.
  3. Run Cantoria: Check Setup from the command palette (Ctrl+Shift+P) to see that all is well.
EngineWhat it isSings
VOICEVOX One installer, many voices. The quickest start Japanese
DiffSinger Voices published one by one by their authors, as folders Japanese, and English, Chinese or Korean where the voice has a dictionary for them
NEUTRINO One download with a voice in it; more voices from its site. Windows only Japanese

VOICEVOX

  1. Download VOICEVOX from its site and install it. Read its terms: each character has terms of its own, and a published work credits VOICEVOX:name.
  2. Run Cantoria: Choose VOICEVOX Folder… and choose the folder VOICEVOX was installed in, the one that holds vv-engine. Cantoria then starts the engine when one of its voices is needed and stops it when VS Code closes. You need not open VOICEVOX yourself.
  3. Write a voice: singer "voicevox/ずんだもん-ノーマル" on melody. The list after singer " shows every voice it has.

DiffSinger

  1. Make a folder for voices, C:\voices for one.
  2. Download a voice in the OpenUtau format from its author, and read the author's terms. Unpack it into that folder, so that the voice is a folder of its own holding a dsconfig.yaml: C:\voices\TIGER\dsconfig.yaml. OpenUtau's Singers folder, if you have one, is such a folder already.
  3. Run Cantoria: Choose Voices Folder… (DiffSinger) and choose C:\voices.
  4. If the voice has no dsvocoder folder of its own, it needs a vocoder, the part that turns what the voice computes into sound. Download the .oudep file of the one the voice's readme names (for nsf_hifigan, the usual one, nsf_hifigan_44.1k_hop512_128bin_2024.02.oudep) from the DiffSinger community vocoders, rename it to .zip, unpack it into a folder, and choose that folder with Cantoria: Choose Vocoder Folder…. The vocoders have terms of their own, in the NOTICE.txt they come with.
  5. Write the voice by its folder's name: singer "diffsinger/TIGER" on melody.

NEUTRINO

  1. Download NEUTRINO (Tau, v3.2 or later) from its site and unpack it. Cantoria drives it on Windows only. It is free under its own terms. It comes with the voice MERROW; other voices from the same site go under its model folder.
  2. Run Cantoria: Choose NEUTRINO Folder… and choose the folder that holds bin, model and settings.
  3. Write a voice: singer "neutrino/MERROW" on melody.

A voice is named engine/name: neutrino/MERROW, diffsinger/TIGER, voicevox/ずんだもん-ノーマル. Cantoria: Choose Default Female Voice… and Choose Default Male Voice… pick the voices used when a score does not name one.

A first song

Four statements, one job each. The part names its words. lyrics holds them. A lyrics row in a score prints them. A singer line sings them.

The singer line stands at the top level of the file, on a line of its own, and its order is fixed: singer WHO on PART sings WORDS. Here the part has said which words are its own, so the line needs no name for them: singer female on melody. Without a singer line the words are only printed, and the part plays its instrument. The piano has none, so it plays as usual, and the voice sings over it.

language english says what the words are written in, and mel- o- dy is one word in three syllables. English needs a voice that sings it: a DiffSinger voice with an English dictionary. singer female takes the default female voice for the words' language; to name one, write singer "diffsinger/TIGER" on melody.

Press ▶. The first time takes a moment while the engine loads its models. Until the voice is ready, the part's instrument plays in its place.

Whose words they are

Say it on the part: lyrics NAME in the part's header, with no braces. It defines nothing; it names the track that is this part's. This is the standard way, and the one place you can read it off the part itself.

part sop { instrument soprano  lyrics words }
part ten { instrument baritone lyrics mw }

singer female on sop     // sings the words the part names
singer male on ten

The same statement in a file written part by part, where each part holds its own sections: it stands with the header's properties, before the first section.

part sop {
  instrument soprano
  lyrics words
  section Verse { c'4 d' e' f' | }
}
lyrics words {
  section Verse { la la la la | }
}
singer female on sop

A file may also say it only by where a score puts the row (staff sop lyrics words: the row under a staff is that staff's) or by a singer line's on PART, and it works. But those say it for one score or for one voice. Once the part names its words, a singer line or a row that gives that part some other track is warned about, which catches a part printing one set of words and singing another. A part may list several tracks; a singer line then sings the one in its language, or the one it names. In one score a part sings one set of words in a section: a verse in Japanese and a chorus in English is written with in Verse on one line and in Chorus on the other.

With the caret on a singer line or on the head of a lyrics block whose part does not say it yet, the lightbulb (Ctrl+.) offers Name these words on the part and writes it for you.

Choose who sings

WrittenWho sings
singer sings wordsthe default voice for the part: a man's for a bass, baritone or tenor part, otherwise a woman's
singer female sings wordsthe default female voice (male for the default male voice)
singer "neutrino/MERROW" sings wordsthat voice
singer male "neutrino/SOMA" sings wordsthat voice; the word before it narrows what the editor offers to voices of that gender

A voice this computer does not have is replaced by the default voice of the part's gender, and Lily# Studio says so once, so a score you share still sings for someone with other voices.

A choir takes one line a part. Two lines with the same words on one part sing it together, and the voices may come from different engines:

singer female "diffsinger/Nishiren" on sop sings words
singer female "voicevox/ずんだもん-ノーマル" on sop sings words
singer male "neutrino/SOMA" on bas sings words

A line inside a score { } takes the place of top-level lines in that score: a named singer's line replaces that singer's, and the others go on; a line with no name replaces every line for its part. So one score can sing the Japanese words and another the English ones.

One song in two languages

Three things make it. Each section writes both sets of words, the second one saying its own language. The part names both tracks, and a singer line that names no words sings the ones in the singer's language: the file's, unless the line says another, as it says a gender (singer alice female japanese …). Who sings which section, and harmony

A band says: this section she sings, this one he sings, and here a third voice comes in above. A singer line says the same, after its part:

Written after the partWhat the voice sings
in Verse Bridgeonly those sections, every time they are played. With no in, the whole part
up 3 / down 6a third above, a sixth below, along the key: the harmony a singer finds by ear. From 2 to 15
octave -1an octave below (1 to 3 octaves, either way)

They come in any order, each once. Several lines on one part sing together, each with its own sections and its own interval:

Lily# moves the notes before any voice sees them, so every engine sings a harmony alike. The third follows the key in force where the note is, a key change included. A note outside the scale is answered with the scale's own note: over g-sharp in A minor, a third above is b. Where that is not the harmony you want, write the line out as a part of its own.

Where none of a part's lines sings a section, the part's instrument plays it: a voice that comes in for the chorus alone leaves the verse to the instrument.

Print what a singer sings

The page shows the part as written; the harmony above is only heard. To print it, give the singer a name — a word before the gender or the voice — and name the singer in the score:

A singer's name stands where a part's or a track's does. staff carol is the staff of what carol sings: the notes of her lines, moved as they say, in the sections they say, and whole bars of rest elsewhere. lyrics carol is the words she sings. A score prints what it says, so you choose: above, carol's staff has no words (they are the lead's, printed once) and bob's has his. A row of a singer's words can also stand without her staff, under the lead's.

A singer who sings an octave below throughout is written in the treble clef an octave down, so the notes stand where the part's do. Such a score sings what it shows. A sheet for one singer is a score of its own: score carol { staff carol lyrics carol }. In a score that has singer lines of its own — the Japanese score of One song in two languages — a singer's staff and words are the ones those lines say.

Lines of one name are one singer. Write the voice on one of them (singer alice female "diffsinger/…" on lead in Verse) and leave it off the others (singer alice on lead up 3 in Chorus). A singer cannot have a part's name: in a score that name is the part.

Where a third is not the note, write the harmony out and mend it. On a named singer's line the light bulb offers Write carol's notes out as a part: the notes become a part called carol beside the lead, her words a track of her own, and her lines one line that sings it. For an instrument — a second violin a third under the first — the command Lily#: Write a Harmony Part… asks which part, how far, which sections and the new part's name, and writes it the same way.

How a voice sings

Leave this out and a voice sings as it does by itself, which is usually what you want. To change it, write with at the end of the singer line and, after it, a name and a value for each setting, with no commas:

singer "diffsinger/Nishiren/2P" on melody with breathiness 3 gender -0.2

The names are the engine's own, so what you can write depends on the voice. The editor lists what the line's voice takes, each with its range, and marks a name the voice does not take.

EngineSettings
DiffSingerwhat the voice's model was trained with, commonly breathiness, tension, voicing (each added to what the voice does by itself), gender, velocity
NEUTRINOsupport: another NEUTRINO voice by name, as a support model
VOICEVOXnone

Inside the music the same names go on a note, and hold from that note on as a dynamic does:

c'4@sing(breathiness 6) d' e' f' |      // from here on
g'2@sing(breathiness to 9) r2 |          // arrives at 9 with this note, gliding from 6
c'4@sing(breathiness default) d' e' f' | // back to the singer line's value

A setting that an engine takes once for the whole voice, such as support, belongs on the singer line. transpose is not a setting: to sing higher or lower than the part, use up, down or octave above.

Write words a voice can read

For Japanese, write kana. A kanji word prints as written but is sung only with a reading glued to it in square brackets: 漢[かん] prints 漢 and sings かん.

Words are japanese unless the file says otherwise, with a line of its own for the whole file or on a track. The names are japanese, english, chinese, korean and latin (Latin is printed and checked; no voice sings it yet). A line no installed voice can sing is played on its part's instrument, and the editor says why once; lysc check --singer cantoria song.lys lists which singer lines have a voice on this machine. With the caret on a language, Ctrl+Shift+Up and Ctrl+Shift+Down change it.

language english
lyrics ja language japanese { さ く ら | }

Slurs, ties and melismas are read as they are for printing: see Lyrics in the reference. @breath on a sung note is a breath, and dynamics, accents and hairpins set how loud the voice is.

Voice, instrument, or both

A part a singer line sings is heard as its voice, and any other part as its instrument. A staff can say otherwise for its score:

Written in the scoreWhat you hear
staff sopthe voice if the part has a singer, otherwise the instrument
staff sop sounds bothvoice and instrument together
staff sop sounds instrumentthe instrument only
staff sop hiddensounds as usual, and the staff is not drawn: a sung line the page does not show

Only a part with a staff or tab line in the score sounds.

Humming and note names

Two track names need no lyrics { } block, because they are made from the notes:

singer female on sop sings hum        // hums on ん
singer female on alt sings solfege    // sings the note names
score { staff alt  lyrics solfege }   // prints the names under the notes

Solfege is movable do, in the file's language.

In the editor

  • The line writes itself. Type singer and completion lists the installed voices that sing the words' language. Choose one and sings is written, with the file's lyrics listed; choose the words and on is written, with the parts. After the part come up, down, octave and in (the sections by name), then with and the settings the voice takes, with values to begin from.
  • The step keys. Ctrl+Shift+Up and Ctrl+Shift+Down change what the caret is on: the voice on its name, male and female, the words on sings, the number of up 3 or octave -1, and the number of a setting.
  • Hear a setting. With the caret in a line's with …, and at each step of one of its numbers, the voice says its short phrase as the line has it sing.
  • Hear a voice. Click a singer line and the voice says a short phrase, at the pitch of the line's gender. Ctrl+Shift+Up and Ctrl+Shift+Down go through the voices, each heard as you reach it; Ctrl+Shift+PageUp and PageDown go to the first voice of another engine. While the piece plays, a click stays silent.
  • The microphone over ▶ is grey and still while a voice is not sung yet, pulses while the engine sings it, and turns blue when it is ready. The bar under it runs from empty to full over what has to be sung anew. A Play meanwhile plays the part's instrument, and a voice that becomes ready while the piece plays comes in at the next note it sings.
  • Edits sing only what changed. About a second and a half after you stop typing, the engine sings the phrases you changed and keeps the rest. A phrase ends at a rest. If it was still singing an older text, it finishes the phrase it is on and moves on; pressing ▶ skips the wait. On a slow computer, set lilysharp.playback.singerPrepare to onPlay so that the voice is sung only when you press ▶.
  • The practice menu has a line for each voice, with a switch, a fader and a microphone of its own (Practice).
  • What is kept. Sung phrases stay on disk between sessions, up to cantoria.cacheSizeMB (1000 MB by default), the ones unused longest going first. Cantoria: Forget Rendered Voices clears them.

Export the song

The preview's Export as WAV mixes the voices in with the instruments. From the command line, lysc wav does the same when it is given a cantoria program (Cantoria's repository has it), with a sound font for the instruments:

lysc wav --soundfont GeneralUser.sf2 --singer cantoria song.lys

Voices and their terms

Each voice is its author's work under its author's terms, and Lily# and Cantoria bundle none. Read those terms before you publish what a voice sang. VOICEVOX has terms of its own, each character has its own, and a published work credits VOICEVOX:name.
