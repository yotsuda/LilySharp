# From a song to a dance

Four steps, each in its own program, all of them text you can read and change:

1. **The song** — a Lily# score with words and a `singer` line. Copilot's chat writes it:
   **Compose with AI…** for a new piece, or **Write a Song over These Chords…** to add a sung
   part to a score you have. A score read from a recording (New File… → Recording) has the
   recording's bars and chords; the song is written over them, and the recording plays under it.
   **Arrange an Accompaniment…** writes the score's own band from its chords: the recording then goes
   silent — its bars still keep the time — and the song can be published.
2. **The voice** — Cantoria, a free extension, has a singing engine on this computer sing the
   words. Nothing is sent anywhere.
3. **The dance** — Shosa, a free extension, writes movement as text the way Lily# writes music.
   Its **Add a Dance…** puts a `.shosa` file beside the score, with a character for each singing
   part; Copilot's chat writes the steps to the words, and the character's mouth sings them.
4. **The video** — Shosa's preview plays the dance with the song, and exports it as an MP4,
   wide or tall for Shorts.

When the chat gives a score its song, Lily# says what comes next. These steps open again with
**Lily#: From a Song to a Dance (Steps)**.
